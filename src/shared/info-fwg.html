Fresh water generator

Introduction

A fresh water generator (FWG), also called an evaporator, makes distilled water from sea water at sea. It runs on the waste heat in the main engine jacket cooling water, so the water costs almost no extra fuel.

Purpose

  • Supply distilled water for boiler feed, engine cooling top-up and technical use.
  • Supply domestic water after the distillate is mineralised and disinfected.
  • Reduce the fresh water a ship must take in port.

Principle of operation

Water boils at a lower temperature when the pressure is lower. At about 90 % vacuum, sea water boils near 45 °C, so engine jacket water at about 80 °C can boil it. Boiling this cool also keeps scale formation low.

  1. Vacuum: the ejector pump drives sea water through the brine and air ejector. The jet pulls air and brine out of the chamber.
  2. Feed: a measured flow of sea water enters the evaporator, about three times the amount of distillate made. The extra water carries the salt away.
  3. Evaporation: jacket water heats the feed through plates or tubes. Part of it boils.
  4. Separation: vapour rises through a demister that catches salty droplets.
  5. Condensation: vapour condenses on surfaces cooled by sea water, giving pure distillate.
  6. Extraction: the fresh water pump sends distillate to the tank. The ejector removes the remaining brine overboard.

Salinity control

A salinometer checks the distillate continuously. If salinity passes the alarm level, a solenoid valve dumps the water instead of sending it to the tank, and the alarm sounds. Normal distillate is under about 10 ppm.

Why heat follows the sea

Boiling temperature follows the condenser, and the condenser follows sea water temperature. Output depends on the temperature gap between jacket water and boiling. Scale, fouling, air leaks and low jacket flow all shrink that gap or the heat transfer.

Safety and good practice

  • Do not make water close to land or in polluted water. Many makers and flag rules say 20 NM from the coast.
  • Start the vacuum before heat, and take the heat away before stopping the pump.
  • Do not trust one salinometer reading. Take a hand sample if in doubt.
