Oil purifier

Introduction

Marine heavy fuel and lube oil arrive with water, catalytic fines, sand, rust and sludge. An oil purifier is a high speed centrifuge that removes these before the oil reaches the engine. Most cargo ships carry two or more for fuel and one for lube oil.

Purpose

  • Remove water and solid dirt from fuel, normally on its way from the settling tank to the daily service tank.
  • Protect fuel pumps, injectors, piston rings and liners from abrasive wear.
  • Keep lube oil clean while the engine runs.

Principle of operation

In a settling tank, gravity slowly separates water and dirt from oil because they are heavier. A purifier does the same job with centrifugal force thousands of times stronger than gravity, so it takes seconds instead of days. The bowl spins at about 7,000 to 12,000 rpm. Oil enters the centre and flows through a stack of conical discs. The narrow gaps give a large settling surface and a short distance for each particle to travel.

  • Heavy dirt is thrown outward to the sludge space at the bowl wall.
  • Water forms a layer between the dirt and the oil.
  • Clean oil moves inward and leaves at the top.

Separation improves with lower viscosity, a larger density difference and a slower flow, as Stokes' law describes. This is why fuel is heated to about 98 °C and why the flow should be as low as the plant allows.

The interface

The boundary between oil and water must stay in the right place. On a conventional purifier the gravity disc sets it, chosen for the fuel density. Too large a disc and oil is lost with the water. Too small and water goes out with the oil. Alcap and G-HIDENS systems need no disc change: they detect the water and discharge it, so they can treat denser fuel.

Operating cycle

  1. Start: the motor brings the bowl up to rated speed through a clutch, while current falls back to normal.
  2. Close the bowl: operating water lifts the sliding bowl bottom.
  3. Seal: water forms the liquid seal so oil cannot escape at the water outlet.
  4. Separate: heated oil is fed in. Sludge and water collect.
  5. Discharge: feed stops, displacement water pushes out the remaining oil, then the bowl opens for an instant and the sludge shoots out.
  6. Repeat: the bowl closes and the cycle restarts, normally every one to four hours.

Suction strainers

Two strainers sit before the feed pump, one in service and one standing by. When the main strainer blocks, flow drops. Change over to the bypass strainer so the purifier keeps running, clean the main strainer, then change back.
