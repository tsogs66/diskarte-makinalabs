Marine boiler

Introduction

A marine auxiliary (or composite) boiler makes steam for hotel services, cargo heating, fuel heating and tank cleaning. On many ships the same drum takes heat from an oil burner and from the main-engine exhaust through an exhaust-gas (EG) section.

Purpose

  • Raise and hold steam pressure for the consumers that need it.
  • Use exhaust-gas heat when the main engine is loaded, so oil firing can stay low or off.
  • Keep boiler water chemistry in band so tubes stay clean and condensate returns safely.

Principle of operation

Fuel oil burns under the furnace; heat turns water in the drum into steam. Exhaust gas from the main engine can add heat through an EG bank or economiser section into the same steam space. Steam leaves through the stop valve to the plant. Extra steam that the plant cannot take is dumped so pressure does not climb.

  1. Feed: distillate make-up fills the cascade (hotwell). The feed pump lifts water into the drum. If the make-up pump air-binds, bleed the casing — restart alone will not restore flow.
  2. Firing: FD fan and FO pump run; FO must be warm enough for the grade (viscosity gate). The burner sequence is purge → pilot → main.
  3. Pressure control: a pressure PID trims burner load. Dump Auto is a separate PID that opens the dump valve against its own set-point.
  4. EG: with the engine loaded, EG steam rises. Some makers (for example SAACKE when fitted) need a circulating pump before the EG section is in service — dry-run protection trips the circ if water is unsafe.
  5. Water: tests drive dose and blowdown. Chloride is never “dosed away”; high Cl means blow down and find the leak.

Shrink and swell

Indicated drum level moves with steam bubbles and pressure, not only with mass. Steam demand up → pressure dips → level can swell. Demand down or pressure rising fast → shrink. The model shows this so you do not chase the glass with the feed valve alone.

Local actions

Soot blow uses main steam: open the steam stop and have pressure up, then blow. A sooted exhaust-gas bank shows high gas ΔP and less EG steam; after the blow, ΔP falls and EG t/h comes back. A dirty FO filter drops FO pressure while the pump is running — change over to the clean filter. These are plant actions on the local panel, not hidden maker menus.

Inspection tests stay in force until you acknowledge the alarm, clear the test cause, and reset a lockout. The PASS log stays unavailable until that path is finished.
